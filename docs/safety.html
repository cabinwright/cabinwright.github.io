Safety and undo

Changing your head unit is at your own risk. Here is what Cabinwright changes, and how to put it back.

What it changes

The store installs apps and its own background helper. Nothing changes the head unit's system files until you turn on a tweak that says so, and the store tells you before installing anything that does.

Every system change first checks the file is exactly the factory one, and keeps a copy of the original.

How to undo it

  • Turn a tweak's switch off and the original files go back, byte for byte.
  • Remove an app or a tweak pack in the store. Removing a pack turns its tweaks off.
  • Some changes show only after the head unit restarts.

If you can't reach the Tweaks app

There is a USB rescue that doesn't need the screen. A restore script on a NEMESIS USB stick puts back the factory SystemUI (what Skip start-up screen changes), and another puts back the factory launcher (what Edit Menu's launcher change replaces).

Scripts: systemui/restore-systemui.sh in tweaks, launcher/restore-launcher.sh in menu.

ADB over Wi-Fi has no password

Anyone who joins the car's Wi-Fi can control the head unit. The head unit's ADB can't ask for a key, so there is no way to limit it to your computer. Turn it on only while you need it.

Only install apps you trust

Today, any app installed through NEMESIS can get full control of the head unit. That is how NEMESIS works, not something the store can stop yet (see the roadmap). Apps in the store are reviewed, and the list is signed so it can't be swapped on the way to your car.
